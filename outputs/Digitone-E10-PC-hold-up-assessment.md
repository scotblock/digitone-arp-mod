# Closed-unit assessment of the shutdown power margin

17 October 2026. The user chose to keep the Digitone closed, without oscilloscope measurements. No hardware operation, power interruption, firmware build or installation is performed here.

## What this establishes

E10 does not enlarge the retained image or add shutdown storage commands. The stock shutdown writer remains byte-identical, with a fixed 0x2a7514-byte logical image. Its fixed 32-KB transfers write 85 chunks of image/padding and one chunk invalidating the old bank. Old-header handling also reads one chunk. Total payload movement is 2,850,816 bytes: 2,818,048 written and 32,768 read. The new E10 records occupy existing serialized space.

This narrows the question to the inherited shutdown mechanism and any interaction of E10 with its entry/update timing. It does not establish a safety margin for either stock or E10.

## Conditional lower bounds, not hold-up requirements

The PC startup snapshot has PROCTL=0x22, SYSCTL=0x000e010c and MISCCR=0x0800. These captured values decode to a 4-bit bus, oscillator-source selection and a card-clock division factor of two. The physical oscillator frequency has not been measured, and these direct-MAIN snapshots do not establish inherited hardware bootstrap state. The field definitions come from [NXP MCF5441x Reference Manual, sections 10.3.4, 25.3.8 and 25.3.9](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/reference-manual/MCF54418RM.pdf#page=635).

For a hypothetical card clock f, payload-only time is `2,850,816 × 8 / (4 × f)` seconds:

| Hypothetical card clock | Payload-only minimum |
| --- | --- |
| 10 MHz | 570.163 ms |
| 12.5 MHz | 456.131 ms |
| 20 MHz | 285.082 ms |
| 25 MHz | 228.065 ms |

These are sensitivity examples, not measured Digitone frequencies or recommended settings. Commands, protocol framing, CPU copies, memory/cache effects and eMMC programming/busy time add to them. The present emulator completes controller payloads immediately, so its elapsed time cannot supply a shutdown upper bound. In particular, 456 ms is not a demonstrated sufficient power reserve.

## Corrections and limits

The earlier “final power latch” description was too specific. The terminal instruction at 0x400a8082 writes 0x80 to 0xec090000, which is the reset controller's software-reset request, not evidence of a board power-hold latch. Other nearby writes configure peripheral pin multiplexing. This identifies register operations; it does not establish the physical power-control circuit. See [NXP reset controller RCR, Table 12-3](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/reference-manual/MCF54418RM.pdf#page=261).

NXP also specifies that edge-sensitive level-7 requests are nonmaskable, including when SR's interrupt mask is 7. Thus an interrupt-masked E10 update must not be assumed protected from the external level-7 input. The shutdown writer is dispatched at level 5, so actual handoff/stack behavior matters. The completed-write scenario previously tested is not coverage of an input arriving partway through each custom retained-image update. See [NXP section 17.1](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/reference-manual/MCF54418RM.pdf#page=333). This is an additional audit limit, not a demonstrated failure in this analysis.

No usable hold-up capacitance, load current, rail minimum voltage, physical input threshold or actual eMMC programming time has been established for this unit. The [Elektron PSU article checked](https://support.elektron.se/support/solutions/articles/43000541580-elektron-power-supplies-psu) does not provide a shutdown hold-up specification. Residual LEDs, an audio click or an external MIDI recording do not observe the internal storage-completion/rail-valid interval.

## What would settle the physical question

The required condition is that CPU, DDR and eMMC remain within their operating limits from the real trigger until required writes are durably complete, with margin. Manufacturer board-level specifications could establish that budget; otherwise it requires suitable internal measurements by a technician. A closed-unit functional power-off/restart check can demonstrate that one attempt recovered, but cannot measure the margin or guarantee interrupted-write recovery. We have not performed or requested abrupt power removal.

With the current equipment constraint, the honest result is **unknown physical hold-up margin**, not a further PC-test pass. The existing partial-write counterexample remains relevant: shutdown must complete, or the current stock selector can accept a partial image. The targeted work still possible without opening the unit is checking external level-7 arrival during the custom update/handoff; that would improve firmware evidence but would not measure retained power.
